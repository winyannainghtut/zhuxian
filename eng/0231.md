# Chapter 231 - Return Home

The clouds dispersed, the rain ceased, and the oppressive darkness of the night finally passed. The first faint light of dawn broke across the horizon, quietly spilling over the mortal realm.

Mount Qing Yun, Big Bamboo Peak, remained enveloped in serene tranquility. Diligent though the disciples were, none would rise this early. Outside the Hall of Observed Silence, lingering dew drops from the night prior clung to the eaves and glazed tiles, dripping intermittently onto the flagstones below. In the distance, the bamboo grove was as lush and emerald as ever; veiled within the morning mist, it drifted gently like a gossamer shroud.

The grand doors of the Hall of Observed Silence stood wide open, just as they did on any other day. Beyond the threshold, above the green flagstones, yellow silken banners hung loosely beside the pillars, while the eternal lamps before the altar of the Three Pure Ones burned quietly in the morning light.

A cool morning breeze blew in from afar, sweeping across the pavilions and courtyards, swirling gently before the hall before venturing further into the distance. Within the wind came the crisp, melodious chirping of birds—the solitary sound heralding the new day.

It was an exquisite picture of peace, a Taoist immortal sanctuary that had greeted countless mornings in this exact fashion, unblemished by worldly dust. Yet on this particular dawn, a discordant, heart-rending anomaly broke the tranquility.

A figure, drenched from head to toe, knelt prostrate outside the entrance of the Hall of Observed Silence, his forehead pressed deeply against the ground in the crook of his arms. The earth around him was thoroughly soaked by water dripping from his frame, and moisture continued to seep and slide from his soaked garments without cease.

Six feet before him, upon the green stone steps before the entrance, lay the quiet, lifeless body of Tian Buyi. Though bereft of vitality, Tian Buyi appeared remarkably peaceful, his features free of agony, as if death were of little consequence to him.

Tian Buyi’s hands were folded neatly across his chest, and his clothing had been meticulously straightened and arranged. His garments also bore signs of dampness, but far less so than the kneeling figure below; muddy stains marred his robes here and there, evidence of hasty attempts to wash and clean them, yet impossible to fully purge in the frantic rush.

Yet Tian Buyi would hardly mind such things.

The morning breeze continued to blow, caressing the summit of Big Bamboo Peak, brushing past the eaves and tiles of the Hall of Observed Silence. As though feeling the chill in the air, Ghost Li’s body trembled slightly. Weak and battered, he remained frozen in that prostrate kowtow toward the hall doors, his head buried in the earth.

Here lay everything he held dear and familiar: the halls, the stone steps, the divine statues, even the scent of the damp earth beneath his knees—fragments etched into the deepest recesses of his soul, never to be forgotten for a single second. Countless times, he had dreamed of returning to the past, of stepping once more onto this mountain soil. And now, he had returned, yet his heart was reduced to dead ashes.

Behind Ghost Li’s kneeling silhouette, across a wide clearing, lay Zhang Xiaofan’s former sanctuary—the kitchen. Ten years had passed, and the two-plank wooden door seemed unchanged, bearing only a few more scars and missing splinters, looking slightly more weathered by time.

The kitchen door was unlatched, and soon, a furry paw pushed it open. With several faint chirps, the monkey Xiao Hui leapt inside. Even the interior layout remained identical: the dining table and benches, the cooking stove, the woks and bowls, all sat in their familiar places. Xiao Hui’s eyes darted about before it skillfully hopped onto the center table and looked toward the right.

Sure enough, to the right of the table against the wall was a large mound of dry straw, upon which a yellow figure lay in deep slumber, snorting softly—none other than Da Huang, who had grown up alongside Xiao Hui.

Xiao Hui perched on the table, curling its tail, but did not immediately jump down to embrace its long-parted companion. Scratching its head, it glanced out the door, then looked back at the slumbering dog, seemingly hesitant.

At that moment, Da Huang, whose ears had been flopped down in sleep, suddenly twitched. His ears pricked upright as though catching a faint sound. Stirring, he opened his eyes.

The familiar silhouette crouching upon the table met his gaze. Da Huang blinked in surprise, then erupted with wild energy, sleepiness instantly forgotten! He leapt up joyfully, barking twice at Xiao Hui, bounded forward on his hind legs, and planted his forepaws on the table edge, tail wagging like a blur.

Xiao Hui grinned, infected by the dog’s enthusiasm. Reaching out, the monkey hugged Da Huang’s head, stroking his sleek, glossy fur. Da Huang nuzzled Xiao Hui eagerly, licking the monkey's face. Xiao Hui chattered with laughter, tumbling off the table. After a brief scuffle of joyous roughhousing, Xiao Hui seemed to remember something. Its brow furrowed, and it patted Da Huang’s head, pointing outside toward the front hall.

Da Huang tilted his head, not quite understanding. Xiao Hui chattered urgently and hopped onto the dog’s back. Da Huang bounded out of the kitchen, looking around, and immediately spotted the silhouette kneeling before the Hall of Observed Silence.

A silhouette that was equally, unmistakably familiar.

Excitement surged through Da Huang. Barking happily, he sprinted across the clearing, his tail wagging furiously. Yet as he crossed the open ground and drew near, his bounding steps suddenly faltered, grinding to an abrupt halt.

His gaze had passed over the kneeling Ghost Li to land upon the peaceful, motionless body of Tian Buyi lying on the stone steps.

Xiao Hui slipped noiselessly from Da Huang’s back, scampered to Ghost Li’s side, scratched its head, looked around, and sat quietly on the ground, leaning closely against Ghost Li.

Da Huang walked forward slowly, ascending the steps until he stood beside Tian Buyi. He stared at his master’s face for a long time, then gently sniffed his body, his tail wagging with a faint, hesitant sway. Finally, looking utterly perplexed, Da Huang nudged his head against Tian Buyi’s cheek, emitting a soft, questioning whine.

Tian Buyi gave no response.

Da Huang stood frozen for a long time. There was no furious barking, no mournful howling. He nudged his master’s cold face one last time, receiving only silence. As though relinquishing all hope, the yellow dog lay down quietly before Tian Buyi. His eyes remained fixed on his master, as if praying he would suddenly awaken. Resting his chin upon his forepaws with drooping ears, he nestled against his master's cold, lifeless body.

The morning wind, carrying the chill of the night, swept past. Below the steps, Ghost Li shivered again, but quickly returned to his motionless vigil, kneeling with head pressed to the earth.

In the chill of the morning, time quietly slipped away.

***

"Ah!"

With a soft cry, Su Ru woke with a start from a nightmare, drenched in cold sweat.

Her hair slightly disheveled and her face weary, she slowly pushed herself up from the table where she had quietly fallen asleep the night before.

The closed window had drifted slightly ajar, allowing the first rays of morning light to filter through the gap. Su Ru stared blankly at the light for a long time until her racing heart settled. Forcing a self-deprecating smile, she pulled the small bronze mirror toward her.

Reflected within was her graceful, breathtaking countenance. Though marked by exhaustion from worry and vigil, her natural elegance remained undiminished.

*The face has not aged, but what of the heart?*

Gazing at her reflection, she sighed, placed the mirror face-down, and rose to open the window fully.

Brilliant morning sunshine flooded the chamber, driving away all gloom and lifting her spirits. A gentle smile surfaced on Su Ru’s face as she stretched languidly against the window sill.

The morning breeze brushed her cheeks, carrying a hint of lingering chill.

Opening the door, she walked outside.

Seeing how early the hour was, she knew the disciples were likely still asleep. Better to let them rest a while longer; she would soon task them with descending the mountain to search for Buyi, which would surely tire them out.

With these thoughts in mind, she strolled toward the front courtyard of the Hall of Observed Silence.

The winding corridor stretched beneath her feet, the slender bamboos rustling in the breeze beyond the railings. For some reason, on this quiet morning, Su Ru noticed many details she had overlooked for years.

The lacquer on the corridor railings was old and peeling in many places. She recalled that the last time the Hall of Observed Silence had been repainted was back during her wedding to Tian Buyi. Without realizing it, this very walkway had accompanied them through countless years, yet she had passed it daily without notice. *When Buyi returns, I must have him find time to repaint it all.*

And on the thickest bamboo outside the railing, one could still faintly discern the faint carvings of two small swords. In the joyful bliss of their newlyweds days, she had carved them into the green bamboo, wishing for their twin swords to harmonize in joint immortal cultivation. Back then, Tian Buyi had teased her for carving them so clumsily, and she had feigned fury, throwing him into an absolute panic until he coaxed her for half the day before she forgave him.

The scenes of youth were still so vivid in her mind. A tender smile touched Su Ru’s lips, her spirits considerably brightened. She took a deep breath of the sweet morning air and continued along the corridor. She then remembered that Da Huang had been raised by Buyi since puppyhood; with his master away for so many days, she wondered if the disciples had fed him properly. If the dog had lost weight, Buyi would surely grumble upon his return.

Smiling with fond exasperation, Su Ru decided to check on Da Huang in the kitchen before the others woke. Walking and reminiscing, she unconsciously stepped into the front hall of the Hall of Observed Silence.

*Clang!*

The first deep toll of the morning bell reverberated from afar—the signal that awakened Mount Qing Yun for a new day. The heavy, resonant toll echoed through the mountain peaks, lingering without end.

Su Ru’s heart seemed to skip a beat in unison with the chime.

Before the Hall of Observed Silence, silhouettes knelt or lay upon the ground. And Da Huang, who was notorious for sleeping late, had risen uncharacteristically early today, lying lethargically upon the stone steps.

Hearing footsteps, Da Huang’s drooping ears twitched. He turned his head and peered into the hall. Standing in the shadow where the dawn light had yet to reach was a woman, staring frozen at the scene.

Su Ru’s heart began to hammer with terrifying speed, threatening to burst from her chest, suffocating her breath. That motionless figure resting quietly upon the stone steps was an image etched so deeply into her soul that nothing could ever erase it.

Yet in her heart, she prayed a thousand times over that she was wrong, that her eyes were deceiving her...

Her face turned as white as paper. Her legs felt filled with lead as she took one trembling step after another, her lips quivering violently. Da Huang watched her approach, wagging his tail faintly once before burying his head back into the ground, staring mournfully at his silent master.

She drew near. She was finally so close that reality could no longer be denied. Tian Buyi’s familiar face filled her vision; he looked as though he had simply fallen asleep, peacefully resting.

The world seemed to spin violently. Su Ru stumbled and nearly collapsed, barely catching herself with her deep cultivation. Even so, her vision blurred with darkness as her strength deserted her, and she sank to her knees beside Tian Buyi’s body.

Her trembling hand slowly caressed his frame and garments. When her fingers brushed over his chest, her hand froze, trembling violently. Tears welled in her eyes, cascading down her cheeks drop by drop, splashing onto Tian Buyi’s cold face.

Beside her, Da Huang let out a whimpering whine, nuzzling his head against her legs.

Slowly, she lifted her head, looking at the figure kneeling below the steps and the grey monkey beside him. After a long silence, she spoke in a low, trembling whisper:

"Are you... Xiao Fan?"

Ghost Li’s body shook. He did not look up; instead, he buried his forehead even deeper, pressing his face firmly against the coarse ground. The gravel scraped against his skin, yet he felt no pain. Only after several moments did his trembling voice emerge:

"It is... this disciple... Shi... niang."

Su Ru offered a heartbroken smile. "There is no need for this. Stand up and speak."

Ghost Li remained prostrate, lacking the courage to look at her, whispering brokenly, "This disciple deserves ten thousand deaths... I failed... failed to protect Master..." Every word felt like agonizing torture tearing through his soul.

Su Ru gently lifted Tian Buyi’s upper body, cradling him in her embrace. Her frame trembled—whether from the chill radiating from his corpse, or from a desperate desire to warm this frozen body with her own heat.

"Stand up," she said, her voice hollow and desolate. In all of Ghost Li’s memories, he had never heard Su Ru speak in such a frail, helpless tone. That realization brought him only sharper agony, driving him to grind his face into the dirt to distract from the heart tearing apart inside his chest.

"If you do not stand, how can you tell me what happened?" Su Ru said softly, her gaze locked exclusively upon the lifeless body in her arms, as though her eyes held room for nothing else in heaven and earth. Da Huang crawled forward two paces, resting his muzzle against Tian Buyi's arm, whining piteously.

Ghost Li paused, then slowly straightened his back, lifting his head to look at Su Ru. That dignified, beautiful woman had not lost her grace, even in this moment of soul-crushing despair. In the morning breeze, strands of hair danced across her temples as she pressed her pale cheek against Tian Buyi’s cold face.

"You have returned... You have finally come home..."

Those were the last words Ghost Li heard from her lips.

In the next instant, the vitality and blood in his chest violently roiled like a stormy sea. His vision plunged into pitch darkness, as though the single bowstring that had been stretched to its absolute breaking point within his mind had finally snapped.

With a heavy thud, he collapsed like a plank onto the stone ground, losing consciousness completely.

In the final haze before oblivion took him, feeling as if his exterior burned like fire while his inside was frozen in ice, he vaguely heard frantic shouts echoing in the distance. The cries carried terror and unbearable agony, quickly melting into widespread weeping.

Frantic footsteps converged from all directions.

*Shiniang! Shiniang...*

That silent, desperate plea was the last thought that flickered in Ghost Li’s mind before the darkness swallowed him whole.
