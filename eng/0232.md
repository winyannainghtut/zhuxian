# Chapter 232 - Loved Ones

In this deep slumber, he did not know how long he had slept. Yet within his rest, he sensed familiar scents all around him. He could not remember how long it had been since he had felt such peaceful reassurance.

Thus, he sank deeply into the dream, seemingly unwilling to wake. Yet in the depths of the dream, a stinging pain lingered, refusing to dissipate, pricking his heart at every turn.

Exhaling a long breath, Ghost Li slowly woke up. Finding himself in this room, he gazed around in silence as though caught in a dream. During his boyhood, he had lived here, grown up here; the table, the bed, the chairs, the door and window frames—virtually every inch was engraved into his heart.

On the wall beside the bed, that large character "Tao" still hung in its place. Though the ink and color had faded somewhat with the years, each stroke remained as vigorous and forceful as the day he had first seen it.

A soft clatter came from the window frame, parting a slit, and the grey-furred monkey Xiao Hui leapt in from outside. Seeing Ghost Li awake and sitting up in bed, the monkey broke into an ecstatic grin, chattering without end as it scrambled onto the bed.

Ghost Li’s heart leaped. This scene felt identical to many years ago; were it not for the wounds upon his body and the third eye opened upon Xiao Hui’s forehead, he would truly have believed all the past ten years had been an illusion.

Yet that was an impossibility.

Xiao Hui chattered at Ghost Li. Looking down, Ghost Li saw that the monkey's paws were filled with wild fruits plucked from the mountainside, seemingly wanting to share them with its master. Ghost Li shook his head, indicating he had no appetite. Xiao Hui did not press him, turning and hopping onto the center table, sitting down to munch greedily on the fruit.

Ghost Li silently took in everything in the room, his gaze finally resting on the window Xiao Hui had left slightly ajar. A sliver of morning light filtered in. Even without looking, Ghost Li knew that beyond the window lay a small courtyard with a green pine, a verdant lawn, a pebble pathway, and a semi-circular archway. Every blade of grass and tree had long been carved into his soul, impossible to erase.

The air smelled sweet and fresh, and even the courtyard seemed to waft the gentle fragrance of greenery.

In his daze, he felt as if he had come home. Yet a moment later, a sharp pang in his chest brought him back to reality.

Footsteps sounded outside the door.

Ghost Li turned toward the door. The footsteps reached the threshold, yet before the half-closed wooden door, the person hesitated, not opening it immediately.

Ghost Li watched the door.

Moments later, the door was gently pushed open.

A tall, sturdy figure stood in the doorway. Almost simultaneously, the man spotted the awakened Ghost Li. Their gazes met in midair, neither speaking for several moments. Countless complex emotions swirled in their eyes, dissolving ten thousand words into absolute silence.

Perched on the table, Xiao Hui spat out a fruit pit, glanced at the doorway, chattered twice, and returned to eating.

The man at the door sighed softly, a bitter smile curling at his lips as he shook his head. Walking in, he looked deeply at Ghost Li and said:

"After not seeing each other for so many years, should I call you Old Seventh, or Little Junior Brother?"

Ghost Li’s lips twitched. Looking at the man before him, he called out softly:

"Eldest Senior Brother..."

Everything on Big Bamboo Peak was as tranquil as memory recalled, so quiet one wondered where the others had gone.

Song Daren stared silently at the man before him. Once upon a time, this had been his most beloved Little Junior Brother, the most unassuming seventh disciple under Master Tian Buyi. And now, time had shifted, and people had changed.

Ten years had passed, and this was their very first reunion.

"Over these years... have you been well?" Song Daren asked, taking a seat opposite Ghost Li.

Ghost Li did not answer, remaining silent. Ten years. Looking back, time had flowed like water, carrying him across so much of the road, yet how could any of it be called "well"?

Song Daren examined him. The boy Zhang Xiaofan of the past still lingered in his facial contours, yet his features were etched with the weariness of the world. And without realizing it, this man who was far younger than himself—and whose cultivation now far surpassed his own—already had faint streaks of white hair at his temples.

Song Daren let out a long, heavy sigh. "How is your body feeling now?"

Ghost Li looked down at his chest. The torn cloth strips from before had been replaced with neat, clean bandages, evidently freshly dressed by his senior brothers of Big Bamboo Peak. Though his chest still throbbed with dull pain, it was vastly better than before he lost consciousness. He murmured, "I am in no great danger now. Thank you for your concern, Senior Brother."

At this point, as if recalling something, he looked at Song Daren. "I... have already rebelled against Qing Yun. Do you all still acknowledge me as a junior brother?"

Song Daren smiled, though bitterness tinged his expression. "Shiniang has already told us everything. When Master was still alive..." Speaking the words "still alive," Song Daren’s eyes reddened, his voice trembling with grief. Hearing it, Ghost Li shuddered.

Steeling himself, Song Daren continued, "When Master was alive, he told Shiniang many times that he had never personally expelled you from Big Bamboo Peak, nor did he ever believe you were at fault ten years ago. Thus, Shiniang instructed us that today, as long as you are still willing, you remain the seventh disciple of Big Bamboo Peak... our Little Junior Brother..."

Ghost Li slowly lowered his head, his body trembling. His left hand clenched the bedsheet into a tight ball, while his right covered his eyes, quietly wiping away the tears leaking from the corners.

The room fell into silence. Only after a long while, seeing Ghost Li gradually regain his composure, did Song Daren speak in a low voice:

"If your body can manage it, come with me to the Hall of Observed Silence. Shiniang is keeping vigil for Master there... She wishes to see you."

"...Yes."

Stepping through the arched gateway, the familiar circular corridor came into view. Song Daren walked ahead without a word, his broad shoulders and back resembling a sturdy hill.

Ghost Li followed quietly behind, remembering his boyhood when he had first arrived at Big Bamboo Peak, following behind Song Daren to slowly assimilate into this world.

Looking back on the past, it felt like an illusion.

His gaze fell upon Song Daren’s waist, only then noticing that a strip of white mourning cloth was tied there, worn in grief for their master Tian Buyi.

Ghost Li’s eyes darkened, and he closed them.

Emerging from the corridor, the Hall of Observed Silence appeared in the distance. Yet unlike its usual pristine quiet, smoke from incense curled from the hall today, accompanied by faint, choked weeping.

Song Daren walked toward the hall. After a couple of steps, sensing something amiss, he looked back and saw Ghost Li frozen in place, staring at the hall without advancing.

"What is the matter?"

Ghost Li’s face was deathly pale. Watching the hall draped in incense smoke and sorrowful cries, a profound terror seized his heart—like a child who had committed a grave wrong, terrified to face the grieving parent.

Song Daren seemed to understand. He sighed, patted Ghost Li's shoulder, and said gently, "Come." Ghost Li stirred, glanced at Song Daren, nodded silently, and stepped forward.

The closer they drew to the hall, the heavier the scent of incense grew, and the clearer the weeping became. Familiar voices echoed within, yet there was no woman’s weeping—neither Su Ru’s nor his Senior Sister Tian Ling'er’s.

Guided by Song Daren, Ghost Li stood once more at the grand entrance of the Hall of Observed Silence.

Eight gazes snapped toward him simultaneously, locking upon his form. Ghost Li trembled, his eyes sweeping across each person in turn.

Wu Dayi, Zheng Dali, He Dazhi, Lv Daxin, Du Bishu!

Those familiar faces presented themselves before Ghost Li’s eyes. Years ago, they had been his dearest kin in the world, the senior brothers he trusted above all others.

Around their waists, like Song Daren, were tied strips of white mourning cloth. Grief was etched across their faces, some with eyes red and swollen from weeping. Within the hall sat a large iron brazier burning with flames, into which the disciples slowly tossed paper money.

Smoke drifted; incense billowed.

Ghost Li stared blankly. Beyond the haze of smoke, Tian Buyi lay peacefully upon a bier. His soiled clothes had been replaced with a clean, neat robe, making his countenance look far more tranquil. Su Ru sat beside Tian Buyi’s body, clutching his cold hand tightly in her own.

Her expression was laden with grief, yet not a single tear fell from her eyes. In her hair rested a small white wildflower, fresh with the morning dew, delicate and laden with sorrow. She simply held her husband's hand, gazing unblinkingly at his face. Her daughter, Tian Ling'er, was not present in the hall.

And Da Huang, raised by Tian Buyi from puppyhood, lay quietly on the floor beside the bier, his head resting dispiritedly against the flagstones, completely drained of his usual playful energy.

Once Ghost Li’s gaze fell upon Tian Buyi, it could not move away. Dragging his heavy steps, he slowly moved forward. Song Daren walked to the side, returned with a hemp mourning rope, and handed it to Ghost Li. Ghost Li looked at him, gratitude flashing in his eyes as he nodded and took it, murmuring, "Thank you."

Song Daren looked toward Su Ru and said, "Go to Shiniang." With that, he walked back to kneel among his fellow disciples before Tian Buyi’s body, kowtowing three times. When he lifted his head, his eyes were red again as he took a stack of paper money from Wu Dayi and tossed it into the brazier.

Ghost Li looked at the hemp rope for a long time, then tied it around his waist. The grey-white rope bound him in mourning, carrying profound sorrow, yet seemingly binding his heart back to this sanctuary.

He walked forward silently, knelt before the bier, and kowtowed three times before Tian Buyi’s body. Then, turning toward Su Ru, he pressed his forehead to the ground.

"This disciple..." His voice caught in his throat. Only after a long while did he speak again in a low, trembling tone: "This disciple Zhang... Xiaofan, pays respects to Shiniang."

Behind him, Song Daren and the five disciples looked over with complex expressions, yet beneath the sorrow was the warmth of blood-deep kinship.

Even upon Su Ru’s face, a faint flicker of comfort surfaced. She looked at Ghost Li, nodded, and then turned with sorrow to Tian Buyi, whispering softly:

"Buyi, do you hear? It is Old Seventh. He has returned to kowtow to you."

Ghost Li remained prostrate at Su Ru’s feet, unable to utter a word.

From behind came the sound of choked sobs.

Incense smoke curled lazily through the Hall of Observed Silence. Without its master, the vast hall felt hollow and desolate, devoid of noise despite the crowd.

After a long while, Song Daren wiped his tears, stepped forward, and whispered to Su Ru, "Shiniang, please give instructions regarding Master’s funeral arrangements. We must notify the elders and masters of each peak, and I also plan to rush to Dragon Head Peak to inform Junior Sister Ling'er so that she..."

"There is no rush for this!" Su Ru interrupted flatly.

Song Daren was startled. The disciples behind him, including Ghost Li, were similarly stunned. The hall fell completely silent.

After a long pause, Song Daren gathered his courage and ventured cautiously, "Shiniang, Master has passed away. We all understand Shiniang’s grief, but the funeral... cannot be delayed."

Su Ru’s expression remained unchanged; in fact, she did not even look at Song Daren. Aside from that single glance at the returned Old Seventh, her eyes held only Tian Buyi.

Song Daren felt awkward, unsure of what to do, looking back at his brothers kneeling by the brazier. They exchanged bewildered looks. Just then, Su Ru called out:

"Daren."

Song Daren answered hastily, "Yes, Shiniang. What do you command?"

Su Ru said, "You and the others step outside for now. Do not enter without my call."

Song Daren hesitated, stepping back a few paces. The disciples looked at him, and the usually clever He Dazhi shook his head with an anxious expression. Seeing this, Song Daren’s brow furrowed even deeper.

Having lived with his brothers for centuries, he knew exactly what He Dazhi feared. He had been with Tian Buyi and Su Ru the longest and understood the boundless love between master and mistress better than anyone. If they all left and Shiniang did something rash in her grief...

At that thought, Song Daren turned pale, his feet rooted to the ground. Seeing their reluctance, Su Ru glared at them, her voice rising in cold anger: "What are you doing? Now that your master is gone, do you no longer respect my words as your Shiniang?!"

*Thud! Thud!*

One after another, aside from Ghost Li who was already kneeling, Song Daren and the Big Bamboo Peak disciples fell to their knees, kowtowing frantically. Song Daren cried out, "This disciple would never dare! We would never dare!"

Su Ru sighed, deep exhaustion washing over her face as if she lacked the strength to scold them. She waved her hand weakly. "Leave."

Song Daren and the others dared not disobey further. With heavy hearts, they withdrew backward with bitter expressions. Ghost Li bowed to Su Ru and prepared to retreat as well, but after only a few steps, Su Ru spoke:

"Old Seventh, stay. I have questions for you."

Ghost Li paused, halting his steps. Behind him, Song Daren and the others breathed a sigh of relief; as long as someone remained beside Shiniang, no tragic accident should occur. Footsteps receded, and soon all six disciples had exited the hall.

The Hall of Observed Silence fell dead silent, save for the crackling of paper money devoured by flames.

Ghost Li stood quietly in place, his head lowered. After an eternity, Su Ru sighed softly: "Your master was always sharp-tongued but soft-hearted. He was always troubled by the upheaval ten years ago. Though he never spoke of it to me, I could tell that in his heart, he felt deeply apologetic toward you."

Ghost Li’s eyes reddened. Shaking his head vigorously, he cried brokenly, "No! It was this disciple who was unfilial, who failed Master's grace! It was I who wronged Master..." His voice choked with sobs.

Su Ru’s lips twitched. Hearing his tearful words, the sorrow in her heart was stirred, yet she fought back her tears. Looking at Tian Buyi, she said softly, "In your master’s heart, he never considered you an expelled disciple. Do you understand?"

Ghost Li bowed his head. "Yes."

Su Ru said, "Since you have acknowledged him as master once more, go over and burn some paper money for him. Consider it your filial duty. Buyi will surely be pleased..."

Ghost Li gritted his teeth, knelt before Tian Buyi’s body, bowed three times with tears in his eyes, then rose and knelt beside the iron brazier. The flames had dwindled since Song Daren and the others had left. Ghost Li picked up an unopened stack of paper money, unsealed it, and began feeding the sheets into the embers.

Su Ru sat beside Tian Buyi, watching the flickering flames cast dancing shadows across Ghost Li’s face.

Suddenly, she asked: "When your master passed away, were you by his side?"

Ghost Li shuddered. Turning to face Su Ru while remaining on his knees, he whispered, "Yes."

Su Ru stared deeply at him. "Yesterday after you collapsed, I treated your injuries and changed your medicine. I discovered that at the point of your severe trauma, a strand of your master’s unique Caihong Sword Qi lingered in your body—the primary cause of your concussed meridians. How did that happen?"

Ghost Li’s heart leaped into his throat, sweat beading on his palms. After a pause, he murmured, "This disciple's injury was indeed struck by Master's own hand. But..."

At this point, he was at a complete loss. The events of that night had been so bizarre and terrifying that even someone who had weathered a decade of mortal strife found them shocking beyond words, let alone when it concerned the tragic death of the master he revered most.

Su Ru gave a cold snort, her phoenix eyes commanding: "Tell me the honest truth."

Ghost Li dared not meet her gaze. Keeping his head lowered, he slowly began his recounting—from meeting the mysterious figure in the ruins of Grass Temple Village, pursuing him to the derelict morgue outside Heyang City, all the way to Tian Buyi's ultimate demise.

As Su Ru listened, her face grew paler by the minute. When Ghost Li described Tian Buyi’s final moments, all color drained from her skin, her hands gripping Tian Buyi’s cold fingers as if terrified he would vanish.

Finally, Ghost Li whispered, "That is the truth of what transpired. This disciple would never dare deceive Shiniang."

Su Ru’s gaze shifted to Tian Buyi, looking upon that peaceful face. Perhaps in her husband's heart, there were few regrets; he had simply done what he believed was his duty!

She took a deep breath, straightening her posture. Though her soul wished for nothing more than to lie down beside her husband and cast the world aside, she knew the time had not yet come.

"Did you truly see clearly...?" Su Ru’s voice sounded ethereal.

Ghost Li did not catch her meaning at first. "Shiniang, what do you mean?"

Su Ru’s voice was faint: "Was that mysterious person truly Sect Master... Senior Brother Daoxuan?"

Ghost Li took a deep breath and answered firmly, "This disciple saw him with my own eyes. Even if he were burned to ash, I would never mistake him."

Su Ru nodded quietly. Moments later, she asked slowly: "According to your words, when Buyi’s mind was overwhelmed and he struck you down, was it Lu Xueqi of Little Bamboo Peak who slew him?"

Ghost Li’s body shook violently, cold sweat drenching his brow. Gritting his teeth, he answered: "Yes!"

Su Ru did not speak, staring blankly at Ghost Li. Under her gaze, Ghost Li's expression twisted in agony. After a long silence, he pleaded in a low voice, "That... Lu Xueqi, she did it to save me... No, the fault is entirely mine!" He kowtowed to the ground. "Shiniang, ten thousand wrongs belong to this disciple alone! Lu Xueqi, she..."

Su Ru sighed. "Among the disciples of Qing Yun, weren't you closest to her over these years? Even after you joined the Demonic Sect, I heard she never stopped caring for you, defying Senior Sister Shuiyue multiple times and refusing the proposal of Valley Master Yun Yilan of Fenxiang Valley. Is that not so?"

Ghost Li knelt prostrate, his mind in complete chaos. On the night of that tragedy, though he knew Lu Xueqi had acted to save his life, Tian Buyi was the master who had raised him like a father. To watch the Tianya Sword pierce his master's chest with his own eyes... in the depths of his agonizing grief, he had instinctively pushed Lu Xueqi away.

The brief embrace after the southern border calamity had been torn apart by fate's cruelty, opening an even wider chasm between them. Why was heaven so ruthless?

Yet before Su Ru, though his feelings were a tangled mess, he could not bear for Su Ru to hold a grudge against Lu Xueqi. But he also understood that Shiniang’s love for Master surpassed even his own; if he himself could barely endure the horror, how could he ask Shiniang to be magnanimous?

Ghost Li fell silent, lost for words.

The truth was as sharp as a razor, wounding every soul that drew near!

Yet Su Ru’s expression was not as unyielding as Ghost Li had feared. After the initial sorrow, a look of contemplation surfaced. Moments later, she asked:

"You said earlier that before Buyi died, his sanity briefly returned and he recognized you, correct?"

Ghost Li nodded. "Yes."

Su Ru asked, "Did he say anything to you?"

Ghost Li pondered, then whispered, "When Master awoke, he spoke two sentences to me."

Su Ru pressed, "What did he say?"

Ghost Li answered, "Master's first words were strange. He merely repeated three words: 'Don't blame her, don't blame her.' The second was his command that after his death, this disciple must bring his body back to Big Bamboo Peak, deliver it to Shiniang, and convey a message..."

Su Ru’s expression shifted. "What did he want you to tell me?"

Ghost Li whispered, "Master commanded me to tell Shiniang to restrain your grief... not to... not to do anything foolish."

Su Ru froze, tears welling in her eyes as her body swayed precariously, looking thoroughly heartbroken. Seeing her agony, Ghost Li worried desperately, yet dared not approach, kowtowing repeatedly: "Shiniang, restrain your grief!"

After a long while, Su Ru’s voice emerged, slightly calmer: "I am fine. Stand up."

Ghost Li rose to his feet. Su Ru’s face had regained composure, though the sorrow in her eyes was unmistakable.

Silence fell upon the hall once more as Ghost Li added more paper money to the brazier. Then, Su Ru spoke quietly:

"In your heart, do you harbor resentment and blame toward Lu Xueqi for slaying your master?"

Ghost Li was taken aback, unable to answer. But Su Ru was exceptionally perceptive; reading his expression, she understood most of it.

She said calmly, "Do you know what the three words 'Don't blame her' meant when Buyi uttered them before he died?"

Ghost Li froze. "What?"

Su Ru smiled bitterly. "If I am not mistaken, Buyi was willing—in fact, he willingly chose to have Lady Lu Xueqi kill him."

Ghost Li was thunderstruck. "Shiniang, your words...?"

Su Ru let out a long sigh. "Enough. The past is unbearable to recall, yet impossible to erase. The secrets of our previous generation should not burden you younger ones." She turned back to Tian Buyi, seeing his peaceful, sleeping countenance, and murmured softly:

"Buyi, you certainly wanted me to tell him that secret, didn't you...?"
