# Chapter 230 - Wound

Mount Qing Yun, Big Bamboo Peak.

The night was deep and still, save for the distant rustle of the bamboo forest whispering softly in the breeze. The lamps had long since been extinguished, and the disciples of Big Bamboo Peak had all retired to rest. Only behind the Hall of Observed Silence did a solitary lamp continue to flicker in the dark.

A cool night breeze blew gently through the half-open window, emitting a faint whistling sound that made the candle flame on the table sway and gutter.

A fair, slender hand reached out gently to shield the wind. The flame stabilized, casting its warm light once more. Su Ru sat beside the table, looking somewhat languid; though the night was deep, sleep eluded her.

The wind outside continued to blow, occasionally rattling the windowpanes. Su Ru rose and walked slowly to the window, but did not shut it immediately, gazing out into the dark.

The sky was black as ink, the darkest hour before the dawn.

She listened intently, yet within the chill night wind, there was none of the sound she longed to hear.

A faint, bitter smile touched Su Ru’s lips. She gently closed the window and returned to the table. Neither she nor Tian Buyi cared for luxury, and the bedroom contained few ornaments. On the table, aside from a cloth bundle, lay only a small round bronze mirror.

She picked up the mirror. Reflected within was a dignified, strikingly beautiful woman with lustrous dark hair and youthful skin, unblemished by a single wrinkle. She and Tian Buyi had been married for hundreds of years, their deep cultivation granting them eternal youth.

After gazing at her reflection for a moment, Su Ru sighed softly, set the mirror aside, and drew the cloth bundle toward her, unwrapping it.

Inside were the most commonplace items: needle and thread, a piece of cloth, scissors, and chalk... Everyday household items that ordinary mortal women kept to sew clothes for their husbands and children. Su Ru took up the fabric, threaded the needle, and began to sew with delicate care beneath the lamplight.

Yet as she stitched, her gaze grew distant under the candlelight, her thoughts wandering elsewhere as her hands slowed. At that moment, the wind outside surged violently. With a loud *clatter*, the newly closed window was blown wide open!

A blast of cold air rushed in, instantly snuffing out the candle flame.

"Ah!"

A soft gasp escaped Su Ru’s lips in the darkness. She frowned as a sharp prick of pain stung her fingertip. With her level of cultivation, to be pricked by a simple sewing needle was almost comical. Yet in the pitch darkness, chilled by the bleak night wind, an inexplicable heaviness pressed upon her chest like a colossal stone.

She sighed, set down the sewing, and walked back to the window. The scenery outside remained unchanged, yet the husband who had stood beside her to gaze upon it countless times had been gone for far too long.

*When dawn breaks, perhaps I should send Daren and the others down the mountain to search again. Waiting like this is no solution.*

Thinking of Tian Buyi’s face, deep worry weighed upon her heart.

The night was deep.

She gazed into the sky for a long time, her lips trembling with unspoken words. After a long while, she lowered her head, let out a mournful sigh, and closed the window once more.

Outside, the wind grew fiercer.

Fox Majestic Mountain, Ghost King Sect.

In the same deep night, others remained sleepless, though their hearts were worlds apart.

Above the colossal blood pool hidden deep within the mountain's belly, the Ghost King and Mr. Ghost stood side by side. Mr. Ghost was shrouded entirely in black robes, his expression concealed, while the Ghost King’s face beamed with unrestrained exhilaration and triumph.

He showed not the slightest fatigue from the vigil. With his immense cultivation, staying awake for days was effortless; now, his complexion glowed with radiant energy as he stared intently at the blood pool below.

Soaked in oceans of blood, the pool had undergone dramatic changes. The four great spiritual beasts remained submerged; even the Taotie, which had struggled so fiercely before, now lay listlessly in the blood water, drained of all strength.

The blood pool was no longer calm. Huge bubbles continuously welled up from the depths, bursting with rapid pops far faster and more numerous than before. The entire pool seemed to boil as an invisible, colossal power awakened from the abyss. The stench of blood in the chamber was more than ten times stronger than before.

Suspended in midair, the Fulong Ding—the nexus of the Four Divinities Blood Formation—had transformed. Its ancient bronze surface had absorbed immense spiritual energy and demonic power from the beasts and the pool, taking on a translucent, amber-crimson hue that radiated solemn, terrifying majesty.

The mysterious inscriptions carved upon the cauldron were all alight, flickering with eerie brilliance as though coming alive. At the center, within the depictions of the four beasts, the ferocious demon god visage glowed blood-red, greedily devouring the spiritual power coursing through the vessel.

Around the Fulong Ding, misty vortices of air swirled within the enclosed chamber, carrying the thunderous roar of wind and storm. Even standing at a distance, the Ghost King and Mr. Ghost could distinctly feel the terrifying power pulsing within the cauldron—a power that continued to compound without cease.

Like the Ghost King, Mr. Ghost watched the Fulong Ding, yet his gaze was icy and calculating, lacking the Ghost King’s feverish fanaticism.

After watching for a long time, Mr. Ghost spoke slowly: "Everything is proceeding without flaw, matching the inscriptions on the Fulong Ding precisely. At this rate, once the cycle of seven times seven—forty-nine days—is complete, the Four Divinities Blood Formation shall achieve total fruition!"

The Ghost King drew a deep breath, his eyes ablaze with wild ambition. Stepping forward, he threw his head back and roared with laughter: "Good! Good! Good! I can hardly wait!"

Mr. Ghost glanced at him. "Sect Master, be patient. The days ahead are long."

The Ghost King laughed uproariously, turning and clapping a heavy hand onto Mr. Ghost’s shoulder. Mr. Ghost stiffened slightly, a strange light flashing in his eyes, but did not move. Finding nothing amiss, the Ghost King laughed again: "Splendid, splendid!"

Regaining his composure, he turned solemn. "Thank you."

Mr. Ghost bowed slightly. "For this supreme formation to succeed is entirely due to Sect Master’s boundless fortune. Without the divine Fulong Ding, I would have been powerless."

The Ghost King smiled and shook his head. "The Fulong Ding has been our sect's treasure for centuries, yet none could decipher its inscriptions until you lent your aid. Heaven has sent you to me to fulfill my great hegemony!"

Mr. Ghost fell silent for a moment. "Sect Master flatters me."

The Ghost King chuckled, turning back to the glowing cauldron suspended in midair, his eyes burning with renewed greed as his laughter echoed through the cavern.

Mr. Ghost stood silently behind him, watching the Ghost King’s increasingly arrogant demeanor without a word. Anyone familiar with the Ghost King would have been astonished by this uncharacteristic arrogance, for he had always been deep and restrained. Yet Mr. Ghost showed not a hint of surprise.

Whether he had seen it often in private, or his own schemes ran too deep to show, none could tell.

Above the roiling blood pool, amid the choking stench of gore, the Ghost King savored his grand ambitions, his laughter reverberating through the cavern. And behind him, a dark, silent shadow stood motionless.

***

Below Mount Qing Yun.

The eerie black clouds in the sky had dispersed, yet heavy rain continued to fall from the pitch-black sky, drenching the lonely mortal realm.

Across the wilderness, the wind blew fiercely, chilling to the bone. What had been an abandoned morgue hours before was now reduced to total rubble, the earth churned into muddy sludge by the destructive clash of titanic powers.

The night was pitch-black, devoid of light save for a faint wisp of azure radiance flickering in the storm.

Lu Xueqi, who had always cherished cleanliness, paid no heed to her mud-stained white robes. Not far before her lay the peaceful body of Tian Buyi, his eyes closed as if in deep slumber. The wind wailed like weeping in the night.

Ghost Li had yet to awaken. By the faint blue light of Tianya, his face looked as pale as a corpse, his features contorted in agony. Only the faint rise and fall of his chest proved he still lived. He lay cradled in Lu Xueqi’s arms, the sword's radiant barrier shielding them from the lashing rain.

Nearby, the monkey Xiao Hui sat quietly, its fur soaked by the downpour. Shivering in the cold wind, it crept closer to Ghost Li's body.

Lu Xueqi looked at Xiao Hui, gently reached out, and lifted the monkey into the protective circle of Tianya, placing it against Ghost Li’s chest. Xiao Hui chattered softly, then rested its head against Ghost Li, its eyes gazing at the silent body of Tian Buyi.

Was it a dream? An illusion?

It felt like a long, distant dream, yet devoid of any joy—for at its end lay only an inescapable nightmare.

Ghost Li stirred, the sorrow on his pale face deepening. With a groan of pain, he slowly opened his eyes.

There was light—a gentle, azure radiance floating around him.

There was sound—the mournful drumming of wind and rain.

Crouched on his chest, Xiao Hui sat up, looking at him.

Another cold gust blew past.

Ghost Li shivered, and then he saw Lu Xueqi’s eyes—that face as pale as his own, the only soul keeping vigil beside him in this stormy night.

Ghost Li’s lips twitched slightly.

The pain in his chest had subsided considerably. Looking down, he saw seven or eight white cloth strips bound tightly around his torso, clearly torn from clothing. His mind cleared, and he realized the shattered ribs in his chest had been set in place. Yet Tian Buyi’s strike had been devastating; his meridians were severely concussed, and even with Lu Xueqi’s emergency healing, it would take days of recuperation to recover.

At that thought, he instinctively turned his head and immediately saw his master lying in the mud. Words failed him; he lacked even the strength to speak. In the rain, Tian Buyi’s face was slick with moisture, resting quietly in the filthy mud.

Who could have known he would meet such an end?

With a low, raspy groan, Ghost Li rolled out of Lu Xueqi’s arms into the mud, struggling to crawl toward Tian Buyi. Lu Xueqi gasped, instinctively reaching out to catch him. But as her fingers touched him, Ghost Li muttered hoarsely:

"Don't hold me."

Lu Xueqi froze, slowly withdrawing her hand. Her gaze followed him as he left the protective glow of Tianya, crawling forward through the mud with agonizing effort. The cold rain soaked him instantly, muddy sludge coating his clothes.

Xiao Hui scrambled beside him, chirping anxiously and trying to pull his master with small paws, but Ghost Li was too heavy, and the monkey could only chatter in distress.

Finally, Ghost Li reached Tian Buyi’s side. His touch met only cold, lifeless flesh. Clenching his teeth, his body shaking, he gazed at his master like a wandering son returning home, only to find utter desolation.

Raindrops dripped from his face onto Tian Buyi’s stiffened features.

The storm raged harder.

Ghost Li’s eyes fell upon Tian Buyi’s chest. Though his robes had been straightened, the terrifying wound remained shocking to behold. Ghost Li stiffened as if struck by a blade.

Slowly, he turned his head and looked back.

Behind him stood the lonely, desolate figure of Lu Xueqi. In the rain, she met his gaze, her face drained of color, her fists clenched so tightly within her sleeves that her nails dug deep into her palms.

In that fleeting gaze, who could measure the heartbreak between them?

The expression on Ghost Li’s face gradually turned blank. Even the initial agony and grief faded, leaving only hollow numbness. Blankly, he turned back to Tian Buyi. Rain had splashed specks of mud onto his master’s face.

Ghost Li reached out and wiped the mud away. As his fingers touched the icy skin, his hand recoiled as if burned, before reaching out again to gently, meticulously clean every trace of mud and water from Tian Buyi’s face.

Then, supporting himself with his arms, he crawled over his master’s body, using his own chest to shield Tian Buyi from the lashing storm, refusing to let the bitter rain touch him again.

Lu Xueqi watched in silence, making no move to stop him, her lovely face etched with pure sorrow.

"When I was a boy, my family was destroyed..." Ghost Li’s voice drifted through the storm, slow and heavy, as though each word had churned in his heart countless times before finding utterance.

Lu Xueqi stepped closer, but Ghost Li did not move, continuing to shield Tian Buyi.

"It was Master who brought me back to Big Bamboo Peak, who taught me and raised me. His kindness to me... I can never repay in this entire lifetime."

Ghost Li’s body swayed, nearly collapsing from exhaustion and injury. Lu Xueqi’s expression shifted, and she reached out to steady him. But the moment her hand touched him, Ghost Li shifted away, avoiding her touch.

Lu Xueqi’s hand froze in midair.

Ghost Li struggled to lift Tian Buyi’s body, cradling his master’s head against his chest, his face twisted in torment as he murmured repeatedly:

"I can never repay him in this lifetime... never in this lifetime..."

Lu Xueqi’s lips trembled. Her gaze swept across Tian Buyi’s face. Who knew that on this very night, this man had smiled and spoken to her, promising to propose marriage on their behalf, offering her a ray of hope amidst despair?

That sword strike... that fatal wound...

How many souls had been wounded by that single blow?

She smiled with heartbreaking sorrow and turned away, her body swaying precariously. Her brows knit in agony, and she coughed up a mouthful of blood. Crimson droplets splattered across her white robes and the muddy ground, only to be washed away by the relentless rain.

She looked up at the sky. Ice-cold raindrops fell upon her face. The firmament remained as black as ink.

*Isn't dawn near? But why, in this vast world, is there only loneliness, storm, and suffocating darkness?*

Tears welled in the corners of Lu Xueqi’s eyes, quietly sliding down through the rain.
