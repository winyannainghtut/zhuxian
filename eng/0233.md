# Chapter 233 - Blood Omen

Mount Qing Yun, Little Bamboo Peak.

The mountain breeze swept through the lush bamboo groves, raising waves of rustling whispers that echoed in the secluded valleys.

Wenmin looked up at the sky. The heavens were cloudless and azure, so clear they seemed almost transparent. Exhaling a breath, her spirits lifted slightly. Yet she did not slow her pace, crossing the bamboo path until she arrived before the small bamboo cottage where her master, Master Shuiyue, secluded herself for meditation.

Reaching the entrance, she knocked lightly on the doorframe. "Master, I have returned."

Master Shuiyue’s voice drifted out from within: "Is that Min'er? Come in."

Wenmin pushed the door open and stepped inside. The bamboo cottage was not large; upon entering, she saw Master Shuiyue sitting cross-legged on a bamboo daybed, eyes closed in meditation. She stepped to the side and bowed. "Master."

Master Shuiyue slowly opened her eyes. Seeing Wenmin alone, she asked, "What, you did not find her?"

Wenmin nodded. "Yes. I went to Junior Sister Lu's quarters twice today, but she was not there. I asked the other sisters, but none had seen her either. Could it be she had business and descended the mountain?"

Master Shuiyue remained expressionless. "Xueqi has always been prudent. If she were to descend the mountain, she would certainly inform me beforehand. Since you cannot find her, she is most likely..." Her voice paused, as though struck by a realization, before she changed the subject: "Since you cannot find her, let it be. It is nothing urgent anyway. You may retire and attend to your own cultivation."

Wenmin nodded, bowed in respect to Master Shuiyue, and stepped outside, gently shutting the cottage door behind her.

As Wenmin’s footsteps receded into the distance, Master Shuiyue’s perpetually calm countenance slowly softened into an expression of deep contemplation. After a long while, she let out a quiet sigh.

Sunlight filtered through the window, illuminating the exquisite bamboo room with bright warmth. Master Shuiyue quietly rose from the bamboo bed, walked to the door, opened it, and walked out, leaving silence behind in the small room.

The Moon-Viewing Platform was the most remote sanctuary on Little Bamboo Peak. On clear, starry nights when the moon hung high, the scenery here was breathtakingly enchanting. Legend held that on nights of the full moon, celestial lunar radiance cascaded like water; reflected by the white stones of the platform, it could illuminate the entire Little Bamboo Peak mountain range—a true mortal marvel and one of Mount Qing Yun's most famed sights.

Over the past ten years, Lu Xueqi had frequently danced with her sword here in the dead of night beneath the moon. As the mentor who had raised Lu Xueqi from childhood—acting as both master and mother—none understood Lu Xueqi’s heart better than Master Shuiyue. Hearing that Wenmin could not find her, Shuiyue had deduced after brief thought that Lu Xueqi was most likely at this secluded cliff.

Walking along the path, the bamboo grew increasingly dense, leading ever further from the bustling front halls of the peak. Though Shuiyue’s own quarters were secluded, walking along this path while listening to the ceaseless bamboo sea still washed her mind clean of all distractions.

*I wonder if Xueqi loves this place so dearly because of this very tranquility?*

Thinking silently to herself, Master Shuiyue stepped onto the Moon-Viewing Platform. Sure enough, the moment she arrived, she saw that familiar white-robed figure standing silently atop a solitary boulder jutting out over the precipice. The mountain wind howled continuously from the bottomless abyss, whipping Lu Xueqi’s white robes furiously in the gale.

The Tianya Sword rested in her hand, quietly emitting soft azure luminescence and auspicious vapor.

Master Shuiyue watched her back in silence for a long time, complex emotions flickering in her eyes. Only after several moments did she clear her throat softly.

Lu Xueqi immediately sensed the presence behind her. Surprised that anyone would come to this desolate spot in broad daylight, and shocked that someone had approached so closely without her noticing, she whipped around.

Seeing her master, Lu Xueqi froze, then quickly drifted down from the boulder, approaching Master Shuiyue with a respectful bow: "Master, why have you come here?"

Sympathy and heartache softened Master Shuiyue’s eyes. Reaching out, she adjusted Lu Xueqi's collar, saying gently, "The astral winds blowing from this chasm carry severe chills. Though your cultivation is profound, exposing yourself to it for long periods will bring no benefit."

Lu Xueqi lowered her head. "This disciple understands. Thank you for your concern, Master."

Master Shuiyue looked at her, sighed, and asked, "In your heart, do you harbor resentment toward me?"

Lu Xueqi was startled. "Master, why do you say such a thing?"

Master Shuiyue said calmly, "I revealed that secret to you and sent you down the mountain. Who could have known that through fate's cruel twists, you would be forced to slay Big Bamboo Peak’s Uncle-Master Tian while he was controlled by the Zhuxian Sword—and right before *his* very eyes."

Lu Xueqi’s expression darkened, but she slowly shook her head. "Master, say no more. This disciple has thought through everything long ago. This matter was heaven's will, something even Master could never have anticipated. Moreover, at that final moment, though Uncle-Master Tian could not speak, I clearly and distinctly felt his intention. That strike... Uncle-Master Tian wanted me to make it."

Her voice paused, a desolate, self-deprecating bitterness washing over her face as she murmured softly, "As for what lies between him and me... this disciple never harbored any foolish hopes to begin with. Sect rules, the heavy mountains of righteousness—I understand them all too well. Uncle-Master Tian of Big Bamboo Peak was the father-figure who raised him from childhood, someone he revered as his own parent. Now that he died by my hand, if I were in his place, I too would find it impossible to accept."

At this point, she looked up at Master Shuiyue, offering a desolate smile. "Master, do not worry about me. I... I have truly let it go."

A sharp pang struck Master Shuiyue’s heart. With her vast life experience, how could she fail to see through Lu Xueqi’s forced composure? Yet this tragedy was so sudden, so irrevocable, that despite having fiercely opposed her stubborn disciple's forbidden feelings in the past, she could not bear to see her in such agony now. Yet pity was useless. Shuiyue sighed softly and shook her head, saying tenderly, "Xueqi, do not grieve excessively. Do not harm your health."

Lu Xueqi smiled faintly and asked softly, "Master, for you to seek me out in this secluded place, is there some urgent matter?"

Master Shuiyue nodded. "Indeed, there is a matter. Though seemingly minor, it is exceptionally peculiar. After pondering it, I felt you were the most suitable person to handle it."

Lu Xueqi asked, "What is it?"

Master Shuiyue looked at her. "It still concerns that secret. Tell me, after the tragedy occurred that night, did you personally escort Ghost Li and Tian Buyi’s body back to Big Bamboo Peak?"

Hearing the name "Ghost Li," Lu Xueqi's face paled slightly, but she nodded firmly. "Yes. That night, he... that person was severely injured. Though his life was not in danger, carrying Uncle-Master Tian’s body back alone would have been far too strenuous, and the matter could not be delayed. So this disciple escorted them. However, I only brought them to the foot of Big Bamboo Peak; the moment they landed, I departed."

Master Shuiyue nodded. "Indeed. That is precisely where the strangeness lies."

Lu Xueqi was surprised. "What is amiss, Master?"

Master Shuiyue said flatly, "According to your words, Tian Buyi’s body returned to Big Bamboo Peak two full days ago. Yet until today, Big Bamboo Peak has issued not a single mourning announcement."

Lu Xueqi was stunned, her brows knitting tightly.

Master Shuiyue walked to the edge, hands clasped behind her back, looking toward the distant horizon where Big Bamboo Peak loomed indistinctly amidst the clouds. After a while, she said, "Tian Buyi is the Head of Big Bamboo Peak, a titan of our sect. The moment news of his passing spreads, even the Sect Master must attend the memorial. For Big Bamboo Peak to keep it completely secret—is that not profoundly bizarre?"

She paused, then turned back to Lu Xueqi. "Furthermore, I sent someone to Dragon Head Peak this morning under an excuse, only to discover that Tian Ling'er is still there, completely oblivious to her own father's demise."

Lu Xueqi was silent for a long time. "This disciple understands."

Master Shuiyue nodded. "You are exceptionally clever; I need say no more. In truth, I suspect nothing nefarious. Su Ru is my junior sister; we have been like sisters for centuries. More than anything, I worry that in her profound love for her husband, she might act rashly and do something foolish in despair. Yet as long as Big Bamboo Peak does not formally declare mourning, I, as the Head of Little Bamboo Peak, cannot visit uninvited. And with so many dark secrets entwined in this matter, it is inappropriate for others to go. Thus, I can only send you once more."

Lu Xueqi nodded. "This disciple understands. If there are no other commands, I shall set off at once."

Master Shuiyue nodded gently. "Go, and be careful. If anything unexpected occurs, return and inform me immediately."

Lu Xueqi bowed in farewell, then summoned the divine light of the Tianya Sword. In a flash of dazzling azure light and a piercing whistle, she soared into the sky like a blue shooting star.

Watching her hurried departure—so unlike her usual calm, steady demeanor—Master Shuiyue knew that though her stubborn disciple claimed to have let go, her heart had let go of nothing at all.

Shuiyue sighed deeply, shook her head, and walked quietly down the Moon-Viewing Platform.

Thousands of miles away, within Fox Majestic Mountain.

Inside the icy stone chamber, frosty vapor drifted serenely upward. The green-clad maiden lay as peacefully as ever upon the chilled stone slab.

Veiled behind light silk, Aunt You stood alone in the chamber, gazing at Biyao for a long time before letting out a soft sigh filled with helplessness.

Lately, too much helplessness had accumulated in Aunt You’s heart—baffling her, grieving her, and filling her with growing unease.

First, the Ghost King seemed to have transformed into a completely different person. Though he remained resolute and ambitious, his actions were growing increasingly ruthless and bloodthirsty. In just a few days, because of minor disagreements, he had executed several subordinates, including an elder of significant standing within the Ghost King Sect.

In the past, the Ghost King would have laughed off such trivial matters. Aunt You could clearly feel the panic and dread spreading throughout the sect; everyone lived in terror, never knowing if a stray word might cost them their lives.

Even more heartbreaking was that days ago, she had accidentally witnessed the Ghost King and Ghost Li crossing blades. Though it lasted only a few clashes, as the Vermilion Bird—one of the Four Great Holy Envoys of the Ghost King Sect—she saw clearly that an insurmountable rift had opened between the two men.

She looked at Biyao, sleeping quietly on the stone bed. It was for this pure, beloved maiden that the two men had united ten years ago. Why, after ten years, had they deteriorated to such a point?

Aunt You dared not imagine what would happen if those two truly slaughtered each other one day. Yet now, it seemed almost inevitable.

"Men... humph, men!"

Aunt You cursed bitterly in her heart, her thoughts tangled in frustration. Looking back at Biyao, her eyes melted into profound tenderness. This child she had loved like her own daughter... every time she looked upon her, her heart broke anew.

As she stood lost in thought, the heavy stone door of the chamber rumbled open.

Aunt You turned to look. The Ghost King appeared in the doorway, strolling inside.

Seeing Aunt You, the Ghost King nodded calmly. "You are here as well."

Aunt You sneered coldly, staring at him in silence.

The Ghost King frowned, a flicker of irritation flaring in his eyes. Lately, he was remarkably quick to anger, vastly different from his former patience. Yet Aunt You was no ordinary subordinate, and her bond with his daughter was unique, so he had always treated her with deference. He asked, "What is the matter?"

Aunt You scoffed. "Do you still remember you have a daughter lying here?"

The Ghost King frowned. "What nonsense is that? How could I forget? She is my only child."

Aunt You said coldly, "Then tell me: how long has it been since you last came here to visit Biyao?"

The Ghost King froze, momentarily at a loss for words. After a moment, a trace of guilt flickered across his eyes, and he sighed: "My fault. The sect's affairs have been overwhelming, and my mood has been foul, so I visited less."

Aunt You said coldly, "I truly do not understand. Not just you, but that Ghost Li as well—what has happened to the two of you? Both of you have changed so much!" Her voice trailed off in despair.

Yet hearing Ghost Li’s name, the Ghost King’s face darkened instantly. "That insolent brat understands nothing of the grand design! Do not mention him before me!"

Seeing the fury on his face, Aunt You opened her mouth, but a profound wave of weariness washed over her. Shaking her head in resignation, she said, "Fine, fine. Do as you please. Take care of yourselves. I cannot meddle, and I no longer care to."

With that, she turned toward the door. The Ghost King watched her back, seeming about to speak, but held his tongue.

Just as Aunt You reached for the stone door, her hand froze in midair. Almost simultaneously, the Ghost King sensed something, a terrifying gleam flashing in his eyes!

An invisible yet unstoppable colossal power, like a raging tidal wave, surged violently from deep beneath their feet! Both supreme experts changed color at the bizarre force.

Yet Aunt You felt shock, while the Ghost King’s surprise was tinged with fierce ecstasy, his eyes gleaming with mad light.

The mysterious surge crested wave after wave like an endless ocean. Slowly, Aunt You felt the earth trembling beneath her boots, the shaking escalating by the second.

Her face paled. The sheer magnitude of this sudden force was horrifying, far beyond mortal resistance. Turning in dread, she saw the Ghost King staring into space with bizarre, burning eyes, showing no fear at all.

Suddenly, within this impregnable chamber carved from solid mountain granite, several sharp cracking sounds detonated like bursting rocks!

This time, even the Ghost King’s face changed dramatically!

In utter shock, the two looked around and saw several visible fissures cracking across the solid stone walls, small pebbles cascading down as the ground shook with violent intensity.

Fortunately, having vented its fury by tearing the mountain stone, the mysterious power deep beneath the earth began to recede rapidly, soon vanishing into nothingness.

Aunt You stood frozen for a long time, her brows knit tight. Were it not for the shocking cracks etched like blades into the stone walls, she would have believed it was a hallucination.

She turned to the Ghost King, who merely glanced at her before turning his attention back to Biyao.

"Do you know what that was?" Aunt You asked, a dark premonition seizing her heart.

The Ghost King shook his head slowly. "I do not. I shall have men survey the terrain to see if an earthquake occurred."

Aunt You pondered, then said, "That was no earthquake. That surging force carried a chilling, distinct murderous aura. It was definitely not a natural disaster."

The Ghost King fell silent, then said, "I will investigate. You need not concern yourself."

Aunt You stared at his back for a moment, the veil over her face fluttering slightly. Saying nothing more, she turned, pushed open the heavy stone door, and walked out.

The stone door rumbled shut, returning the chamber to silence. Gazing upon the peaceful face of his daughter, the sharp, calculating coldness in the Ghost King’s eyes finally softened.

He sat down beside the ice slab, looking at Biyao with unspeakable sorrow and longing.

It seemed that only in these rare moments, facing his sleeping daughter alone, did he reveal a trace of human frailty.

Yet who would ever know?

Outside the chamber, Aunt You took a few steps along the corridor before stopping, looking around with a deep frown. Inside the insulated chamber, the damage had seemed minor, but out in the wide mountain tunnels of Fox Majestic Mountain, the scene was far more severe.

The vast network of tunnels was strewn with fallen boulders and shattered rock. In the distance, frantic shouts and groans of pain echoed through the corridors.

Evidently, that mysterious subterranean power had impacted Fox Majestic Mountain far more violently than anticipated.

Amidst the chaos, Aunt You noticed another alarming anomaly: within these well-ventilated mountain tunnels, a faint, metallic scent of blood had begun to permeate the air.

Where did this scent come from? It seemed to hang everywhere, inescapable no matter where she walked. Though not overpowering, the bizarre scent felt profoundly unsettling. Yet burdened by too many sorrows, she had no heart to dwell on it.

Feeling as though a suffocating weight were crushing her chest, she wanted nothing more than to break out of this mountain cavern and breathe the open air.

Aunt You’s figure soon vanished into the depths of the winding tunnels, leaving that faint, ominous scent of blood quietly drifting in the mountain gloom...
